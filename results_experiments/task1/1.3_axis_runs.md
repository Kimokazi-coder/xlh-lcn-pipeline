## Conclusion

- **The lattice is in the raw data.** In the raw red crop the horizontal threads are visible as wavy
  fluorescent lines of varying brightness crossing the vertical canaliculi. The preprocessed image and
  the skeleton follow them; the preprocessing does not create them.
- **It is not an instrument pattern.** There is no lattice-scale spectral peak above noise, in the whole
  image (1.2) or inside the box (top local peaks 17.8 and 17.5 against about 15.5 for noise, at periods
  31 and 19 px, i.e. horizontal threads roughly every 20 to 30 px but irregular). The orientations form two
  broad peaks about 15 degrees wide around 0 and 90 degrees, with no spike at exactly 0 or 90: within
  3 degrees of 0 holds 6.9% of the box's skeleton and within 10 degrees 21.3%, the same density per
  degree. A raster or scan artefact would be locked to the pixel axes. The only pixel-locked content
  (1.2) is 0.1 to 0.2 grey levels at periods of 2 and 4 px.
- **The same box is elevated in all three 682 images** (axis-run share 0.333, 0.349, 0.356 against
  0.21 to 0.23 outside it), and not in the 542 and 543 images. If the 682 images are sections of one
  field (task 6.1), this is the same tissue region seen three times, which also argues for structure.
- **Axis-aligned runs are common everywhere**: 20 to 24% of skeleton pixels in every image against 1.6
  to 2.6% for diagonal runs. The threads in these fields run mostly along x (543) or along y (682), so
  this is tissue anisotropy plus the image axes being roughly aligned with it, not a defect.
- **What the horizontal threads are cannot be settled from these images.** They behave like canaliculi
  running along the lamellae, crossing the radial ones, but lamellar interfaces or another linear
  structure cannot be excluded in 2D. They are counted by the skeleton and so enter field density and,
  near cells, the ring lengths. Recorded under Decisions needed: Karim to judge the crops by eye.
