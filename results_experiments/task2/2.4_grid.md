## Reading

- **Each cut drives different measures.**
  - t_lo (network) moves field density almost one to one: +11.1% median at 0.8, -8.2% at 1.2. It moves
    ring 30 and ring 60 nearly as much, and roots per cell less (+6.2% / -7.4%). It changes no
    lacuna.
  - t_hi (lacunae) moves lacuna count and area: median area +29.9% at 0.8 and -24.7% at 1.2. Through
    the bigger or smaller bodies it moves roots per cell (+18.7% / -12.7%) and ring 30 (+11.7% /
    -10.7%), the size effect of 4.1. It leaves field density within 1.2%.
  - Both together add up: at 0.8, roots +26%, ring 30 +24%, density +10%; at 1.2, -20%, -20%, -8%.
- **Bridges are the least stable output** (-79% to +86% for 10% changes of t_lo): where thread ends
  fall changes with the cut.
- **Lacuna counts change in every image under some t_hi scale.** At 0.8 they range from 31% fewer
  (542_z06, 16 to 11, bodies fuse) to 50% more (682_z08, 10 to 15). Only 542_z06 holds its count at
  1.1 and 1.2; 542_z18 and 543-2 move by one at 0.8 and 1.2.
- **A pooled cut changes the comparison between fields.**
  - The pooled lacuna cut (t_hi = 0.600) is within 7% of each image's own cut except 682_z23 and
    682_z29 (1.11 and 1.15 times); it changes their counts (14 to 13, 13 to 10) and moves roots by
    -11% to +16%.
  - The pooled network cut, the same in raw units for all images, is 1.54 to 1.61 times the default
    in the 543 images and 0.60 to 0.71 times in the 542 images. Field density then becomes
    0.036 in the 543 images (from 0.044 to 0.045) and 0.038 to 0.041 in the 542 images (from 0.034).
    **The ordering of the fields flips**: the 543 field goes from densest to least dense. Roots per
    cell flips the same way (543: 7.6, 6.9, 6.7 to 5.8, 4.1, 5.0; 542: 8.6, 8.3 to 11.3, 11.4).
- **What this means.** The image-relative cuts (the default) make each field's network share similar
  and leave the between-field differences to structure. A pooled cut instead assumes that equal raw
  intensity means equal signal in every image, which holds only if staining and acquisition were the
  same. The 543 images have a brighter red background (2.2, 5.2) and a lower top-hat peak, so the two
  choices disagree most there. Neither is shown to be right without calibration or validation counts;
  this is a Decision needed, and it matters more for between-field comparisons than any other
  setting tested tonight.
